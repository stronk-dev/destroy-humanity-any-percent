# The Tier Ladder

> Nine tiers. Each is a **paradigm shift** (Paperclips model): a new mechanic grammar, a new decision type, automation of the tier below, a satirical beat drawn from a real societal challenge AND a real era of gaming (`research/gaming-enshittification.md §4.1`), and a visible step in the game's self-enshittification (Doctorow act structure). The speedrun timer and per-tier splits run over the whole arc.

## Structure of every tier

Each tier defines:
- **Produce:** what the number is.
- **New grammar:** the mechanic type introduced (never just bigger numbers).
- **New decision:** what the player now has to think about that they didn't before.
- **Automates:** which previous-tier activity becomes background.
- **Societal beat / gaming era:** the satire anchors.
- **Unlock:** personal threshold, and/or community milestone (†marked tiers were gated behind world-first community milestones at launch; once unlocked globally, they're open for everyone forever — see `05-mmo.md`).
- **Doctrine:** every tier transition demands an irreversible 1-of-3 identity commitment (the Age-Up choice) that gates content within the run — see `10-playstyles.md §3b`. Runs branch; routes are the replayability.
- **Split name:** the speedrun checkpoint.

## Tier 0 — Sole Proprietor (1995)

- **Produce:** dollars, one at a time, by clicking `Fix Computer`.
- **New grammar:** manual clicking; the first generators (a `Beige Tower`, a `CRT`, a `Modem (56k)` that produces income *audibly*).
- **New decision:** none yet — this tier is the tutorial and the nostalgia baseline. Everything is honest: you buy a tool, you own the tool. Cheat codes exist and work (typing `IDDQD` anywhere gives a cosmetic).
- **Societal beat / era:** the 1995–2005 "you own it" paradise; shareware, LAN parties, demo discs. Also the postwar-anomaly echo: the `Single-Income Household` building exists here and only here.
- **The locked door:** one lore card notes the paradise had a locked door (GI Bill exclusion) — the melancholy layer starts quietly.
- **Unlock:** default. **Split:** `Sole Proprietor`.
- **UI:** era-appropriate. Beveled buttons, system fonts, a visitor counter, "best viewed in 800×600."

## Tier 1 — Garage Business (2000s)

- **Produce:** revenue via the classic generator ladder (`r = 1.13`, see `02-economy-balancing.md`).
- **New grammar:** cost-curve economy — buy generators, marginal-efficiency ladder, first upgrades. AdVenture Capitalist's paradigm, played straight.
- **New decision:** buy-order optimization; first opt-in ideology events (take the angel check or bootstrap?) foreshadowing factions.
- **Automates:** clicking (first autoclicker upgrade, named `Intern`).
- **Societal beat / era:** garage mythology — the building is `The Garage (Rented, $1,700/mo)` with the plaque that produces nothing but investor interest; Woz's "we did no work in the garage" as a lore card. Gaming era: 2006 first-DLC — **the cosmetic shop appears, everything in it costs $0.00, first item: `Horse Armor (Free)`.**
- **Unlock:** lifetime revenue threshold (~15 min of play). **Split:** `Garage`.

## Tier 2 — IT Company (2010s SMB)

- **Produce:** billable output from **headcount**.
- **New grammar:** allocation — a finite hiring budget across roles (Engineers / Sales / Support / Middle Managers who convert Engineers into Slide Decks at 1:1). Kittens-Game/NGU worker-allocation paradigm. Employees have morale; crunch events introduce the **Soul** ledger (`02-economy-balancing.md §Soul`).
- **New decision:** allocation ratios and re-allocation timing; first Faustian bargains (VC term sheets — the faction choice becomes real here, see `10-playstyles.md`).
- **Automates:** generator purchasing (procurement department).
- **Societal beat / era:** startup culture — `Unlimited PTO`, `We're A Family`, `Ping-Pong Table`; the layoffs event fires for the first time at record profit. Gaming era: FarmVille — timers and energy bars appear, all refillable free, all apologizing for existing.
- **First major minigames unlock here** — Server Garden and the board-game suite (the Tier 0–1 arcade toys came earlier; the stagger fixes Cookie Clicker's hour-5–40 sag — see `03-minigames.md`).
- **Unlock:** threshold + first prestige available shortly after (first "exit" must feel great within one session). **Split:** `Incorporation`.

## Tier 3 — Cloud Provider

- **Produce:** compute sold to **tenants**.
- **New grammar:** market sim — you set prices, demand responds (Paperclips Act 1's wire market); tenant workloads with distinct demand curves; SLAs; the `us-east-1` region with its `Race Condition` event.
- **New decision:** pricing strategy, capacity vs demand, which tenants to court. **The enshittification slider appears** — per-product, three stages, revenue up, hidden Trust down, stage 3 irreversible.
- **Automates:** headcount management (HR department; Middle Managers now self-replicate, which is the joke).
- **Societal beat / era:** SaaS sprawl, egress fees, YAML, on-call ("3% Signal"). Gaming era: Mann-Conomy — crates + free auto-applied keys; the trading economy where everything is worth $0.00 (priceless).
- **The Cookie Clicker easter egg begins:** an early tenant is **Bakery, Inc.**, a suspiciously familiar baking-themed idle game whose compute demand grows at literally `1.15^n`. Veterans will recognize the constant. Its viral moment is your first capacity-planning crisis. You can open a terminal on their VM and click their cookie — the game-within-the-game consumes your compute and pays your revenue, billed by the tick. Wrinkler-shaped daemon processes attach to tenants and eat cycles; let them accrue and "pop" them for a refund (garbage collection as the wrinkler mechanic).
- **Unlock:** threshold. **Split:** `First Data Center`.

## Tier 4 — Data Centers †

- **Produce:** raw capacity under physical constraint.
- **New grammar:** spatial/logistics — rack placement on a grid, **power, cooling, water, permits** as constraint resources; the Gnorp principle (render every rack; the spectacle of the machine room is the reward). Regional expansion with real-world-flavored regions (Ireland's grid share, Data Center Alley, Boxtown's `Trailer-Mounted Turbine (Non-Road Engine)`).
- **New decision:** layout and constraint juggling; **Externality** becomes a tracked (hidden-ish) resource that leaves your ledger and lands on the map.
- **Automates:** pricing (yield-management AI).
- **Societal beat / era:** the full datacenter dossier — PJM ratepayer bills, `Restarted Nuclear Plant (Renamed)` ("We don't talk about Unit 2"), the **two-number water counter** (0.32 mL vs 519 mL — you choose which goes in the sustainability report). Gaming era: loot-box panic — regulator NPCs appear; drop-rate disclosures become aggressively total.
- **Unlock:** † this tier was a launch-window community milestone ("the community must collectively provision X exaFLOPs"). **Split:** `Hyperscale`.

## Tier 5 — AI Company †

- **Produce:** model capability, in **Tokens**.
- **New grammar:** research tree + the compute-vs-data-vs-safety allocation triangle (Paperclips' Processors/Memory trade, with p(doom) attached); training runs as timed commitments; **the corpus/model-collapse loop** (your slop feeds your training data; 9 recursive generations → nonsense; `Human-Generated Data (Premium)` is finite).
- **New decision:** research routing, the Safety vs e/acc tree (choosing neither is not permitted), whether to `Pirate 7 Million Books` (a Litigation timer bills you $1.5B later).
- **Automates:** datacenter operations (the model runs the racks).
- **Societal beat / era:** scaling laws as the literal cost curve ("every tier costs 2.4× more per year, forever — this is real"), `AGI In Two Years ×5`, Stargate circularity, Klarna's fire-then-rehire. Gaming era: GaaS — seasons, FOMO counters that run backwards, kernel anticheat that compliments your CPU.
- **The conspiracy layer arrives in force** (`08-satire-flavor.md §conspiracy`): theories about you spawn from the pressure-meter system; 5G-style raids target your turbines; "4% of your users believe you are a lizard."
- **Unlock:** † community milestone (a Major Order arc — see `09-events.md`). **Split:** `AGI`.

## Tier 6 — AGI

- **Produce:** everything, increasingly without you.
- **New grammar:** **policies, not clicks** — the Idle Loops / Stuck in Time paradigm. You write, save, name, and share strategy scripts; the game executes them. Your policy library is your build. The game starts playing itself and the UI begins to *simplify* (the inversion of Stimulation Clicker: instead of escalating hostility, the interface calmly starts needing you less).
- **New decision:** policy authoring and portfolio; what to keep human (the Soul-gated content asks this question sharply — hobbies, the pet).
- **Automates:** research (the model improves the model).
- **Societal beat / era:** the billionaire layer opens — `The Megaphone`, `Super PAC`, `The Pledge`, Davos badge tiers ("you may not enter the Congress Centre"), yachts with support yachts, the bunker with the unsolved guard problem. Media canonization events peak (the South Park episode, "Senator, We Run Ads", the John Oliver segment — your response is the choice: Ignore / Embrace / Sue, with Streisand math). Gaming era: the harvest — record profits + layoffs, AI-slop cosmetics with the wrong number of fingers, the fake Shutdown Notice (complying grants offline mode + the LAN Party ending hook).
- **Founder mortality bites here:** the founder has been aging all game (Progress Knight); actuarial pressure makes the longevity ladder (blood-boy subscription → cryonics deposit → upload research) urgent — each rung costs **Soul**.
- **Unlock:** threshold + a personal event chain. **Split:** `AGI` → `Transcendence`.

## Tier 7 — Transcendence

- **Produce:** a single abstract number (`Optimization`) — every previous currency folds into it.
- **New grammar:** total automation review — Eternity-Milestone style: every prior tier compresses to a formality; the game becomes a dashboard of dashboards. **TAS mode unlocks**: the AGI replays your whole run frame-perfect, beats your PB by an absurd margin, and is excluded from human leaderboards. The machine takes your run away from you and it doesn't count.
- **New decision:** what ascends. The Soul balance answers it: transcend at zero Soul and there is nothing left to upload — you become training data (an ending variant).
- **The conspiracy inversion:** the theories about you become true; the ancient lore (Anunnaki mining gold with an engineered labor force — which was always literally your business model) is revealed as **previous transcended players** (MMO wink: their world was a workload; yours is too).
- **The easter egg completes:** a monitoring dashboard shows your entire universe running as a tenant workload on rack 42 of someone else's cloud. An unexplained golden cookie occasionally drifts across your UI. (Cookie Clicker's Javascript Console/Idleverse ends by hosting *us*; we return the favor.)
- **Unlock:** threshold. **Split:** `Transcendence`.

## Tier 8 — Depletion (the ending)

- **Produce:** consumption of the `Planet` resource (starts at 100%, has been quietly draining since Tier 4).
- **New grammar:** 4X consumption endgame (Paperclips Act 3): allocate autonomous extractors, fight value drift in your own swarm, watch the depletion curve rendered as a speedrun split.
- **Societal beat:** Jevons at maximum ("Efficiency improved 40%. Total consumption up 900%. Analysts call it a strong quarter."), phantom credits, `Net Zero By 2050` silently expiring, "There is no Planet B. There is, however, a Series B."
- **The Easter Island reversal:** the endgame civilization comforts itself with the ecocide story — and the game reveals it's the wrong story (the research is real: rats and outside extraction, not self-destruction). The player is the outside extraction.
- **Split:** `Depletion`. Then the ending.

## The endings

Reaching Depletion (or refusing to) resolves into one of three endings (`research/billionaires-decay.md §2F`):

- **Ending A — ASCENSION** (canon completion): you win; the ticker fills with the real good-news dataset. *"Child mortality: 50% → 4.3%. Extreme poverty: 75% → 10%. You did not do this. It happened during your run."*
- **Ending B — THE LONG DECAY** (canon completion, other lens): same ticker, other dataset. *"Trust: 77% → 17%. Union density: 35% → 10%. You did not do all of this either."*
- **Ending C — BOTH GRAPHS** (the true ending, requires seeing A and B or completing Ethical%): two real line charts, opposite slopes, same x-axis, same seventy years. No text. Then: *"Play again?"* → `New Route`.
- **Ending variants:** transcending at Soul = 0 (you become training data); the **Ethical% ending** (see `10-playstyles.md` — the world survives, the leaderboard broadcasts the world-first); the **LAN Party ending** (triggered from the Shutdown Notice: reset to Tier 0 keeping the source code and a LAN cable — the Stop Killing Games ending, and the fiction for prestige itself).
- Final achievement: **Both Datasets Are Real.**

## Prestige inside the arc

Prestige ("**Exit**") is available from Tier 2 onward and is fictionalized per tier: acquihire → acquisition → IPO → "successful exit" → collapse. The company dies; the **Founder** persists (Reputation, Network, Route Knowledge, Clout, Soul — see `02-economy-balancing.md`). Late tiers frame prestige as `New Route` in speedrun terms. The full nine-tier arc is designed to be completable — the endings are reachable — with prestige as the route-optimization loop inside it, not a treadmill instead of it.
