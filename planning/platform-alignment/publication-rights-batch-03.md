## File findings

### `design/research/absorption-arena.md` — REVISE

The dated 2026-08-06 research is source-linked and generally clear about `[V]`, `[M]` and `[sim]`.
It is therefore recoverable with a bounded revision. Before tracking publicly:

- label the entire recommended arena, economy hook, tier, multiplier, names and player-facing
  lines as non-adopted research proposals;
- replace categorical “costs exactly the same,” “bots are trivial,” “no cross-runtime burden” and
  “ship this” conclusions with their actual prototype/owner/RFC gates;
- shorten the accumulation of source-derived descriptions and retain only the mechanics needed to
  support the comparison;
- narrow the legal matrix: mechanics/expression is a useful general distinction, not a blanket
  freedom-to-operate finding, and the dossier itself says no patent search was performed;
- refresh stale claims that the arena/lane has already been accepted for a release tier.

### `design/research/board-game-mechanics.md` — REVISE

The comparative mechanic-family structure is useful public research and the source list is clear,
but the file repeatedly moves from evidence to binding-sounding product direction. Before tracking:

- retain the taxonomy and explicitly label bot difficulty, server cost and product-fit rankings as
  hypotheses requiring prototypes rather than measured conclusions;
- remove or verify unfetched exemplar specifics; do not let `[M]` facts accumulate merely because
  each appears on a final verification list;
- relabel exact names, copy, economy hooks, recommended roster additions and “do not build” calls
  as proposals routed to design/owner authority;
- correct stale “already ships” and accepted-surface statements against HEAD;
- avoid treating one cited US copyright case plus an unperformed patent search as a project-wide
  clearance.

### `design/research/lane-pusher-design.md` — PUBLIC SYNTHESIS REQUIRED

This is not merely a literature review. It combines a large amount of vendor-specific mechanics,
community-wiki material, exact commercial performance figures, a non-retained simulator,
unreproduced experiments, product copy, exact constants, a proposed minimum spec and categorical
legal/roadmap conclusions. Its own verification section concedes that the simulations are
falsifiers rather than tuners, sampled only part of the deck space, omit player skill and must be
reimplemented before any shipping constant is trusted.

The public artifact should preserve only the decision-grade synthesis:

- one-lane auto-combat tends to stalemate without bypass/anti-stalemate verbs in the exploratory
  model;
- loadout-only depth produced dominant sampled decks under fixed policies;
- a live player versus a server-driven snapshot opponent avoids an offline victim;
- pets may share data/identity vocabulary without being disposable lane bodies;
- all exact engine, balance, content, copy, legal and roadmap choices remain RFC/owner work.

The raw dossier needs a named private-source store or an explicit verified cleanup decision. A
future public synthesis must link only the short necessary source facts and either retain a
reproducible simulator artifact or describe the simulation as non-reproducible exploratory work.

### `design/research/rhythm-timing-games.md` — PUBLIC SYNTHESIS REQUIRED

The central problem is real—network arrival time cannot honestly establish fine local input
timing—but the proposed solution is not sufficiently evidenced to become public project
architecture. The file acknowledges that key osu!/DDR/StepMania timing, replay, anomaly-detection,
network-jitter and patent claims were not fetched. It nevertheless specifies exact trust,
anti-cheat, production-power, accessibility and cross-runtime behavior.

The public synthesis should retain:

- coarse server-owned timing and fine client-observed timing have different trust boundaries;
- deterministic replay can prove score/replay agreement but cannot prove a physical keypress was
  honest;
- accessibility and latency calibration must be product requirements, not reward penalties;
- precise windows, anti-cheat thresholds, production multipliers and ranked eligibility require
  primary evidence plus adversarial prototypes.

It should refuse the unsupported claim that a client-supplied simulation-tick index is inherently
“hard to forge,” and must not route a rhythm/accessibility assist to a lower production reward
without an explicit accessibility/product ruling. Keep the raw dossier private or clean it up
explicitly after the synthesis is accepted.
